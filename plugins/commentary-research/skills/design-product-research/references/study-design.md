# Study design and Commentary mapping

## Plan

1. State the decision, prioritized research questions, assumptions, and what evidence would change the decision.
2. Define participant groups, inclusion and exclusion criteria, accessibility needs, recruitment, incentives, and sample rationale.
3. Select the method and success or observation criteria without turning exploratory work into a pass/fail test.
4. Prepare consent, privacy, recording, retention, withdrawal, and observer rules.
5. Pilot the protocol and revise confusing or leading material.
6. Define the analysis approach before collection.

For a qualitative usability round, 5–8 realistic participants is a common starting range. Use additional rounds across distinct user groups rather than treating one small sample as statistically representative.

## Commentary workflow

- **Consent:** plain-language purpose, activities, data, observers, recording, use, retention, voluntariness, and withdrawal.
- **Content:** authorized stimulus, context, or concept exposure.
- **Activity:** realistic behavioral task with optional success criteria that are never inferred merely from completion.
- **Form:** structured attitudinal response, post-task measure, or follow-up.
- **Complete:** fixed acknowledgment and exit.

Keep participant sequencing in one ordered workflow. Do not create task wrappers around steps.

## Synthesis

For each finding, record the claim, evidence references, affected participants or segments, frequency, severity, confidence, counterevidence, product implication, and proposed next decision. An agent-generated finding remains a proposal until a human accepts it.
