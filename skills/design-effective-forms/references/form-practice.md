# Form practice rubric

## Questions

- Define the audience, purpose, owner, expected volume, and decision supported.
- Prefer behavior and concrete experience over hypothetical opinion when the decision requires behavioral evidence.
- Avoid leading, loaded, double-barrelled, ambiguous, absolute, or jargon-heavy wording.
- Use open questions for discovery and narrative evidence; use closed questions for consistent routing, aggregation, or comparison.
- Keep scale direction and wording consistent. Preserve exact wording and order when measuring change over time.

## Flow and validation

- Start with low-effort relevant questions; group by topic.
- Branch only to reduce irrelevant work. Put complicated follow-up sections on a separate step.
- Identify optional and required fields before entry.
- Give instructions before failure, associate them with the control, and keep entered values after validation errors.
- For long forms, show the current step and total progress and make optional stages recognizable.

## Pretest

Test the whole form or a coherent question sequence in the actual medium. Use cognitive prompts to learn how respondents interpret questions and usability observation to learn whether they can navigate and complete the flow. Revise from observed problems rather than stakeholder preference alone.
